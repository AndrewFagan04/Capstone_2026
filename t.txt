**I. Essential Data (Absolutely Necessary for Basic Day Trading)**

These are the core data points you *must* have.

*   **Open:** The price at which the stock opened for the current day.  (For strategy initiation)
*   **High:** The highest price the stock reached during the current day. (Defines price range)
*   **Low:** The lowest price the stock reached during the current day. (Defines price range)
*   **Close:** The price at which the stock closed for the current day. (Most common measure of price movement)
*   **Volume:** The number of shares traded during the current day. (Liquidity & momentum indicator; high volume often signals stronger movements)
*   **Adj Close:** Adjusted Close price. *This is crucial.* It accounts for dividends and stock splits, providing a more accurate representation of the stock's true price history.  Using the raw `Close` price can create misleading data for training because it won't reflect the real returns an investor would have experienced.

**How to use these in your program:**

*   **Feature Engineering:** These data points are the building blocks for creating many other features (e.g., daily range = `High` - `Low`, relative strength, moving averages – see "Important Data" below).
*   **Reward/Loss Calculation:** The `Adj Close` price is *essential* for determining profits and losses for your agents.

**II. Important Data (Significantly Enhances Strategy Capabilities)**

These data points will help your agents identify patterns and react more intelligently.

*   **Moving Averages (MA):**  Calculate these yourself using the `Adj Close` data.  Common periods include:
    *   **5-day MA:** Captures short-term trends.
    *   **20-day MA:**  More medium-term trend.
    *   **50-day MA:** Identifies longer-term trends.
    *   **200-day MA:**  Very long-term trend indicator (often used to define bull/bear markets).
*   **Exponential Moving Averages (EMA):** Similar to MAs, but give more weight to recent prices, reacting faster to price changes.  Same periods as above (5, 20, 50, 200).
*   **Relative Strength Index (RSI):**  A momentum oscillator that measures the magnitude of recent price changes to evaluate overbought or oversold conditions.  (Typically a 14-day period is used).  You'll need to calculate this yourself using `Adj Close` data.
*   **MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence):**  A trend-following momentum indicator that shows the relationship between two moving averages of prices. You'll need to calculate this.  Typically involves a 12-day EMA, 26-day EMA, and 9-day EMA.
*   **Bollinger Bands:**  A volatility indicator consisting of a moving average and a band above and below it, calculated using standard deviations of price.  You’ll need to calculate these using the `Adj Close` price.
*   **Volume-Weighted Average Price (VWAP):** A trading benchmark that is typically used by institutions.  Calculated using the `Adj Close` and Volume data.

**How to use these in your program:**

*   **Technical Indicators:** Implement these as features. The agents can learn to use these signals to make trading decisions.
*   **Trend Identification:**  Agents can learn to identify trends by observing moving averages and other trend-following indicators.
*   **Overbought/Oversold Conditions:** RSI and Bollinger Bands can signal potential reversal points.
